🌍 Climate Change & Melting Glaciers

Subtopic: Ice, Water and a Warming World

Earth is warming, ice is melting, and seas are rising—but informed choices today can still protect tomorrow’s water, land, and life.

Climate change is the long-term warming of our planet and shifts in weather patterns, driven mainly by greenhouse gases from burning coal, oil, and gas. Glaciers and polar ice melt, oceans expand, and extreme events grow stronger, affecting people, animals, and water resources everywhere.

Climate Basics

  • Climate is the average of weather over many years; climate change means those long-term patterns are shifting.
  • Today’s warming is mainly caused by human-made greenhouse gases, not just natural cycles.
  • Key gases include carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide, which trap extra heat near Earth’s surface.
  • Even a small rise in global temperature can greatly affect storms, rainfall, ice, and ecosystems.
  • Limiting warming quickly reduces the chance of crossing dangerous climate “tipping points”.

Main Human Causes

  • Burning coal, oil, and gas for electricity, transport, and industry is the largest source of CO₂.
  • Clearing forests removes trees that would absorb carbon, and often releases stored carbon back into the air.
  • Agriculture and livestock add methane and nitrous oxide, powerful heat-trapping gases.
  • Industrial gases and some coolants can warm the climate thousands of times more per kilogram than CO₂.
  • High consumption and waste—fast fashion, short flights, wasted food—push emissions even higher.

Glaciers, Ice & Water

  • Mountain glaciers and polar ice sheets are shrinking as temperatures rise, feeding extra water into rivers and oceans.
  • Sea-level rise comes from melting ice and from seawater expanding as it warms.[web:293][web:296][web:306]
  • Communities near coasts and low-lying islands face more flooding, erosion, and saltwater intrusion.
  • Many rivers in Asia, Europe, and South America depend on glacier melt, so long-term loss threatens water supplies.
  • Protecting ice means cutting emissions fast today, not waiting for future technology alone.

Big-Picture Solutions

  • Switch energy systems from fossil fuels to renewables like solar, wind, and hydropower.
  • Improve efficiency in buildings, transport, and industry to use less energy for the same work.
  • Protect and restore forests, wetlands, and mangroves that naturally store carbon.
  • Redesign cities around public transport, cycling, and walking instead of car-only growth.
  • Support fair climate policies so changes are fast, practical, and just for all communities.

Everyday Climate Actions

  • Walk, cycle, carpool, or use public transport instead of short solo car rides or frequent flights.
  • Save electricity using efficient lights, turning devices fully off, and avoiding unnecessary cooling.
  • Eat more plant-based meals and reduce food waste by planning portions and storing food properly.
  • Use and reuse items longer, repair when possible, and cut single-use plastics.
  • Join local clean-ups, tree-planting, and climate clubs, and talk about climate solutions with friends and family.

Campus & Community Projects

  • Run campus campaigns on saving energy, avoiding plastic, and using public transport, and track how much emissions your school avoids.
  • Start peer-education clubs that explain climate science, myths vs facts, and easy low-carbon habits to younger students.
  • Work with local government or NGOs on tree-planting, wetland restoration, or lake clean-ups that also reduce flood and heat risks.
  • Create simple climate tools—posters, reels, websites, or apps—that help people measure and reduce their own carbon footprint.
  • Use project work to survey how climate change is affecting local water, crops, or health, and present solutions to teachers and leaders.

Why Every Degree – and Every Drop – Matters

Scientists show that each extra fraction of a degree of warming brings more intense heatwaves, heavier rains, faster glacier melt, and higher seas—but quick emission cuts can still limit the worst outcomes. Meeting climate goals needs strong policies plus millions of people choosing cleaner, low-carbon options that also protect water and ice.

1.5 °C
Safer limit compared with 2 °C for coasts, glaciers, and coral reefs.
Net-zero
Cut emissions and balance what remains so extra warming slows.
Cities
Urban transport and buildings can make big emission cuts fast.
Nature
Forests, wetlands, and oceans absorb a large share of CO₂.
Together
Collective action multiplies the impact of each small step.
