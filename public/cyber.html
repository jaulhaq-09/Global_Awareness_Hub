Cyber Security Basics

  • Cyber security protects the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of data and systems (CIA triad).
  • Attackers target passwords, devices, networks, and cloud accounts to steal or damage information.
  • Everyone who uses the internet—students, teachers, families—shares responsibility for staying safe.
  • Most attacks succeed because of small lapses like reused passwords or clicking suspicious links.
  • Good security combines technology (antivirus, updates) with smart daily habits.

Common Cyber Threats

  • Malware: viruses, ransomware, spyware, and trojans that infect devices and lock or steal data.
  • Phishing: fake emails, SMS, or calls that trick you into sharing passwords or OTPs.[web:283][web:289]
  • Account attacks: password guessing, credential stuffing, and brute-force logins on popular sites.
  • Web/app attacks: SQL injection, cross-site scripting, and fake websites that steal information.
  • Denial-of-Service and botnets: massive traffic that makes a site or game server unusable.

Safe Online Habits

  • Use long, unique passwords and a password manager instead of reusing the same one everywhere.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication (2FA) for email, banking, social media, and gaming accounts.
  • Pause before clicking links or attachments, especially from unknown senders or urgent messages.
  • Download apps and software only from official stores or trusted websites.
  • Log out of shared devices and avoid saving passwords on public computers.

Securing Devices & Wi-Fi

  • Keep operating systems, apps, antivirus, and browsers updated to patch known weaknesses.
  • Use screen locks, PINs, or biometrics so others cannot access your phone or laptop easily.
  • Change default router passwords and use strong Wi-Fi encryption like WPA2 or WPA3.
  • Avoid banking or logging into sensitive accounts on open public Wi-Fi; use hotspot or VPN.
  • Back up important notes, documents, and photos so ransomware or device loss does not destroy them.

Privacy & Social Media

  • Limit personal details such as address, phone, and daily location in public profiles.
  • Review privacy settings so only trusted people can see posts, stories, and contact details.
  • Be cautious with quizzes, games, and third-party apps that request excess permissions.
  • Do not share screenshots of IDs, tickets, or bank details on social platforms.
  • Report and block accounts involved in cyberbullying, harassment, or blackmail.

When Something Goes Wrong

  • If you clicked a suspicious link, disconnect from Wi-Fi and run a full antivirus scan.
  • Change passwords for affected accounts and enable 2FA immediately.[web:272][web:283]
  • Take screenshots and keep evidence of scam messages or bullying before blocking.
  • Inform a parent, teacher, or IT/cyber cell rather than hiding the incident.
  • For serious money or data loss, contact your bank and local cyber-crime helpline quickly.
